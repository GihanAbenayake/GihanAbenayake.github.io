My time working with Unreal Engine has led to a range of smaller projects being worked on to familiarise myself with the engine and its capabilities. A few of which I will be listing down below

Project Fracture

Project Fracture is my most current project, taking on themes from the Sandbox Shipbreaking game Hardspace Shipbreaker (HS) and a few sandbox factory builder type games such as Factorio or Satisfactory.

Breaking down what I've wanted to recreate from each game, the overall intention was to combine HS's style of salvaging with a progression akin to building a factory to automate tasks like what could be found in Factorio or Satisfactory.
For HS, The Salvaging gameplay loop was the main thing I aimed to recreate, from being able to slice through ship parts with a pinpoint beam, to carving swathes of the ship apart with a linear cut. Alongside that, I wanted to be able to sort those salvaged parts into materials that could be used to build up the factory to assist in future ship salvaging and so the gameplay loop was born.

The loop for Project Fracture is intended to be:

Select a Ship to Salvage, and cut through and sort what can be taken off by hand -> Sell those materials for a universal currency (Credits) -> Use those credits to purchase gear and drones to assist in salvaging the ship more safely and/or efficiently. -> Sell or Process those more exotic materials for more credits. -> Repeat


As of writing this, the Project is still in development, but the Pinpoint cutting and Slice cutting have been implemented alongside 3d movement, with the next steps being to implement the resources themselves and then factory elements.

The factory elements of the game, are one source of progression, with a focus around drones to assist with salvaging. The player will start out with a single, small Salvage drone, to assist with moving around chunks of the sliced ship. And to collect resources from it. And as they continue to progress they'll gain access to more advanced drones to assist with salvaging. Such things as Cutters to help slice ship parts, or larger Tug drones to move heavier pieces into position. All of those will assist with automating a factory-esque construction that the player builds with purchased resources.

While working on creating this game, I've learned quite a lot about Procedural Meshes, 3D Movement and the implementation of physics onto sliced objects so far.

Project Builder / Logistico

Project Builder, also known as Logistico, is a game inspired by the likes of Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic (W&R), in particular, its unique approach to construction and logistics.

The game uses a setting called Realistic Mode, which, requires a player to not only designate a building to be built, but to also store and ferry resources via vehicles to the build site. While a slow process, it has peaked my interest in the logistics management area, and I decided to pursue making a similar concept in Unreal Engine.

Logistico's aim, is to focus more on the construction aspect, rather then where W&R focuses on the management of a town. With the aim to start in debt, and work your way out by assisting in the completion of contracts and in turn, building up a successsful logistics and construction company.

Logistico's main loop is as such:

Select a Contract -> Deliver the Required Materials ->Deliver the Required Vehicles -> Begin and monitor construction -> On completion gain Credits -> Use those Credits to finance the purchase of Materials to build industry Or Purchase Vehicles to assist in speeding up the construction process or allowing new, more profitable buildings to be made -> Repeat


While working on the project, I've learnt quite a bit about how to use masked materials, as well as learning about procedural generation for terrain using noise textures to simulate height maps for voxel based terrain, similar to Minecrafts own terrain generation. User Interface design has also been something I've delved into with the project, and I'm excited to continue working on it.
